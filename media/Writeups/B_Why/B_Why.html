Why do people still like tube amps?


Despite being over 70 years old, tube amplifiers remain highly sought after, with many people willing to pay thousands of dollars for them, even though cheaper digital and solid-state alternatives exist. This appeal can be attributed to two key reasons:


First, tube amps produce asymmetrical waveforms when they distort, generating even-order harmonics. There’s some debate over whether these harmonics sound more pleasing to the human ear, but personally, I find them appealing.


Second, tube amplifiers offer a remarkable dynamic range. They can transition from a clean tone to strong distortion just by varying how hard you play. This level of responsiveness is difficult to replicate in digital or solid-state amplifiers, where distortion is often introduced through artificial signal clipping.
